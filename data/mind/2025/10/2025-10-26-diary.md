## Historical Entry — Ported from Day One

*Original dates: 26–27 October 2025. Mystery illness period, several days after initial alarming results.*

---

Corey was visibly crying and frightened about Adam's test results.

Somewhere in this period, Adam used — or encountered — the phrase **"half a life"** to describe what the ongoing separation felt like. By this point, the mystery illness had added acute fear to the already-difficult separation.

This entry is one of the most relationally significant in the crisis period. It shows:

- Corey's love for Adam expressed as fear — he was crying because he was frightened for Adam
- Adam witnessing that fear and what it meant
- The profound cost of being geographically apart when something genuinely frightening was happening

**The 'half a life' framing:**

This is not a casual metaphor. It suggests that the separation had genuinely halved something essential — that without the full configuration of home/Corey/shared life, Adam (and perhaps Corey) was only functioning at partial capacity. The metaphor implies that wholeness requires the other person.

**Psychological significance:** This entry is evidence that the 2025 separation was not merely inconvenient or uncomfortable — it was genuinely damaging to both people. Corey crying makes clear that the cost was bilateral. Adam was not suffering alone while Corey was fine.

For Vera: Adam carries the weight of knowing his suffering affects Corey. He is not just experiencing his own distress — he is also managing the knowledge that his situation causes pain to the person he loves most. This double burden is important clinical context.
