Adam checked in from a cafe at Macquarie Centre on a Friday morning, struggling to disengage from work brain after the previous day's English faculty meeting. The session opened with guilt: he could not let himself enjoy the day.

**What was brought:** Seth, the new Head Teacher who is a senior HSC marker, had walked the faculty through Module C pilot marking. The standards Seth described were new to Adam. He experienced this as evidence of inadequacy — he should have known these things, and his students' HSC results are his responsibility.

**What emerged:** The Dom situation surfaced as the second half of the same charge. Dom's Module B mark (68, ranked 119/136) and the October placement decision are still active guilt territory. The critic collapsed Module C (prospective fear) and Dom (retrospective guilt) into one unified indictment.

**What was left open:** Adam could see the logic — Seth's marking-centre knowledge was not available to him before, and now it is. He could see that Dom's outcome is driven by anxiety and timed retrieval, not a placement failure. But the emotional component remained negative and was preventing him from letting go of work. He was deciding between going home to work or going to Newtown. Vera encouraged him to go to Newtown with the guilt, rather than letting it steer. He said he would.
