The session centred on holiday overwhelm that did not match the apparent size of the day's tasks. Adam described brief work tasks, getting pants hemmed, going to Private Blends and returning to university work as carrying a disproportionate weight, with the body registering them as dread, anxiety and exhaustion rather than simple errands. What emerged underneath was not only task avoidance, but a fear that the last year of Crohn's, poor sleep, stress and grief may have altered his capacity in a more durable way.

The important turn came when the question shifted from "what is wrong with me?" to the concrete conditions of sleep and inflammation. Adam could name that the answer may still be unknown because the body has not yet had the conditions required to test recovery. There was also grief here: missing his grandparents, the frustration that sickness and loss do not queue neatly, and the ache of not being able to process life like a checklist.

What was left open was the pressure of "more, more, more": the sense that if Adam is not actively doing, improving, catching up or achieving, then something is being wasted. The session did not resolve that pressure. It located it.

Worth carrying forward: what is the voice of "more" protecting you from feeling when you stop?
