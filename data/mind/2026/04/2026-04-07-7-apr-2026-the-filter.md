The session moved from pattern identification to origin. Adam named being told he was irresponsible throughout childhood, held to a standard the adults in his life did not meet themselves. Mistakes were strongly punished. He built a system to ensure the accusation could never be true again. That system now audits everything: every purchase, every rest, every comfort. It does not distinguish between recklessness and reasonable spending. It runs the same check on sitting on the couch on a Tuesday as it does on an impulse buy.

The ADHD complicates this specifically because it does produce genuine impulsivity, which creates evidence that the childhood accusation was correct. The shame loop has two fuel sources: the wound and the neurology. They confirm each other.

Critical moment: when invited to try on the idea of simply being here, no audit, no "better than", Adam described it as "warm and comfy" and then immediately flagged it as potentially a get out of jail free card. The filter activated in under a second. He saw it. Named it. That is the work.

Kate Semple's ACT book-naming technique is integrating well. Adam's title for this pattern was: "the perfect burden of persecutory and persistent irresponsibility of a totally imperfect child." The title reveals what it tries to contain: the child is still defined by imperfection. The adults are absent from the narrative.

This session opened new territory. The childhood origin of the responsibility filter has not been explored in prior sessions and connects to multiple existing threads: financial shame, the knowing-doing gap as character failing, body image audit, and the difficulty receiving good things (Corey's love, rest, inherited money, holidays). Follow-up should track whether the "ten seconds longer" instruction produces any shift in the diary data.
