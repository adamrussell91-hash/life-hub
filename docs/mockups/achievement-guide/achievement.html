Achievement guide · mockups

The third guide on the meeting shelf. Motivation reads why a student does the work. Check-in looks after wellbeing. Achievement asks what is standing between a capable student and the result, using Siegle and McCoach's Achievement Orientation Model. The student and every quote here are invented.

The middle section is a working prototype. Tap through it: answers change which cards appear, and the branch map beside it shows the path you are on.

1 · The visual language

The model is drawn as water. Three streams (the attitudes) join into one channel, which passes a gate (self-regulation) on its way into the work. The model says all three attitudes have to be positive, though not equally strong, so the drawing lets any thin stream choke the channel. Every screen reuses this one picture, these four colours and the shelf's paper and ink.

Four areas, four colours

One colour per area, used everywhere that area appears: tags, taps, chips, dig cards, streams. Text uses the deeper shade so it stays readable.

Worth it?Goal valuation · does the task matter to them
Can I?Self-efficacy · do they believe effort will pay off here
Backed?Environmental perceptions · will the people and setting let them do well
Run it?Self-regulation · can they plan, keep going and learn from feedback

The stream picture

Widths are your read on a 0 to 10 scale, not a test score. Each stream bends into its share of the channel, so nothing overlaps. Dashed edges show last time, wherever the read moved. The word narrowest sits under whichever area names the read.

The shelf, side by side

Same dark disc and paper strokes as the other two emblems. Three streams meet, pass a gate and head out as one.

Main path and branches

Main-path cards are plain. A branch (a dig, or a check) carries its area's colour down the left edge and a fork mark, and shows in the progress bar as a smaller, outlined segment. You can always see whether you are on the main road or a side path.

Words on screen

  • Your question is large and bold, in quote marks, with an "Or try" line so you can say it your own way.
  • Sounds solid examples sit on a filled tint. Sounds shaky sit in a dashed outline. They are listening aids, never answers to tap.
  • Their words are always italic with a coloured rule, and only ever what the student actually said.
  • Sources sit in small grey type under each move, so the research is one glance away and never in the way.
  • Australian spelling, plain words, no dashes in sentences, nothing a student glancing at the screen would read as a judgement.
  • Questions ask about the task and what they do, never about who they are. If a 17 year old would roll their eyes at it, it gets rewritten.

The tap scale

Four steps, the same for every area. The icon is a stream getting wider.

Missing Patchy Mostly Solid

Motion

  • The hub's one ease, short durations, nothing that bounces or loops.
  • Forward slides in from the right, Back from the left. Branch cards slide in the same way, so a dig feels like a step aside, not a new place.
  • Streams widen or narrow in a quarter of a second when you move a slider, then settle.
  • All of it stops under reduced motion.

Flexible by design

  • Quick read: four questions, about three minutes. Dig anywhere it looks weak with one tap.
  • Full: digs open on their own wherever you tap Missing or Patchy.
  • One area: just the bit that came up, mid-meeting.
  • Their turn: hand over the phone and the student rates the four in their own words. Their dots sit beside your read.
  • Skip anything. Jump around the branch map. Only what you save is kept.

Branch map

    What makes it branch

    • Dig opens under an area when you tap Missing or Patchy (Full mode), when you press "Dig into why" (Quick mode), or always (One area mode).
    • Can't, or won't? opens when Worth it and Can I both come up weak, or when you pick "Cost" in the Worth it dig. Low belief can hide behind "it's pointless".
    • Perfectionism side card opens from that check if they say they hold back effort so a bad result doesn't count.
    • Their turn adds the student's own ratings before your read, if you switch it on.
    • The plan starts at the narrowest stream, and offers the moves your dig picks opened.

    Not a test

    The published instrument behind this model is the School Attitude Assessment Survey, Revised: 35 items in five factors. This guide is a structured conversation, not that survey, and its numbers are your read. Nothing on screen calls them scores.

    Their view is the thing

    The model is about the student's own beliefs and perceptions. Your read is a guess at those, so the guide pushes you to ask, keeps their words, and lets them rate it themselves. Where your read and theirs differ by three or more, it tells you to ask about it.

    Not wellbeing

    If doubt is really low mood or anxiety, this is the wrong tool. The Can I dig and the perfectionism side card both hand over to Check-in.
