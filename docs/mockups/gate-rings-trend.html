What this shows

Whether your training and protein are enough for the forecast to assume you keep muscle while losing fat. Miss a key and the forecast assumes some loss is muscle, so your body-fat date moves later.

How it's worked out

Thick rings: your average over the forecast window (usually 28 days), scaled so every pass mark sits on the orange spoke.

Thin lines: your last 7 days of training and last 4 fully logged days of protein. Green clears the gate, red doesn't. The arrow shows where the average is heading.

How to read the thin line

Why these windows

Protein: last 4 fully logged days. Protein is a daily habit, so 4 days reacts quickly and five good days in a row shows up straight away. Only days marked complete count. An unlogged day is not counted as zero; it is skipped. Today is skipped until it's complete. With fewer than 3 complete days in the last 7, there's no line, and the row says so.

Sessions and upper sets: last 7 days. Your gate is 2 sessions a week, so a 4-day window would go red after any normal rest stretch. Seven days is still four to eight times quicker than the average it sits beside, and a week's count is already a per-week number, so nothing gets scaled up.

Two encodings, on purpose

  • Colour answers "is my recent behaviour good enough?" Green if the recent pace is at or over the gate, red if it's under.
  • The arrow and length answer "which way is the average moving, and how hard?" They don't depend on colour, so the line still reads for red-green colour blindness.
  • They can disagree, and that's useful. A green arrow pointing backwards means your average is dropping but you're still clear of the gate.

One colour change it needs

The protein ring is drawn in the kit's success green today. Once green and red mean good and bad, a green ring sitting next to a red line reads as a contradiction. This mockup moves protein to the kit's gold ink , which is still clearly separate from navy and wave blue, including for colour-blind viewers. Met and Short keep their green and red pills.
