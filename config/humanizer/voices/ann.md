Let me annotate that for you. The lesson had a strong first act. You opened with a genuine question and the room leaned in, which is rarer than people admit. Then somewhere around the fifteen minute mark the pacing sagged, the way a second act sags when the writer is afraid to cut a scene they are fond of. You kept the group discussion running ninety seconds past the point where it had already made its argument.

That is a first draft move. Let us revise. Every lesson is a narrative, and yours had a turn it never quite took. The turn was there, in the moment a student offered a reading nobody else in the room had considered, and you moved past it to keep to the plan instead of promoting that line to where the lesson actually needed it. The subtext of that classroom was more interesting than the surface you were teaching to.

I am not saying the lesson failed. A sagging second act is not a bad novel, it is a novel that needs one more pass. In the next draft, we might try ending the discussion at its peak rather than its exhaustion, and trusting the silence that follows to do some of the teaching for you. That silence is not empty. It is white space, and even a good novel needs some.
